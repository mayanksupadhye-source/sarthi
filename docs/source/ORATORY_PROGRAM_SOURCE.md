## Document text

13-WEEK ORATORY PROGRAM

One topic. One skill. One recording cycle. Every Sunday.

13 speeches   •   13 recordings   •   13 feedback loops   •   13 deliberate improvements

THE JOURNEY, AT A GLANCE

Each week stacks a new oratory skill on top of everything before it. By Week 13, you are not learning something new — you are running every skill at once. Here is the arc:

What This Actually Takes

2 hours, every Sunday, 12:30–2:30 PM, for 13 consecutive weeks — the fixed format on page 2 never changes.

Two recordings per week, every week: one raw first attempt, one corrected final attempt. Thirteen weeks means 26 recordings on file by the end.

One skill at a time. You are not asked to be great at gestures, voice, pauses, and movement simultaneously until Week 13 forces it.

Visible discomfort in weeks 1–2, 5–6, and 11 in particular — those are the weeks where the skill itself (being on camera, silence, arguing against yourself) is uncomfortable by design.

No restarting the cycle if a week is missed — resume at the next week (see the Week 7 note). Continuity, not a perfect streak, is what the program is built around.

By Week 13: every individual skill from the previous 12 weeks runs together in one full performance, scored against the entire arc from Week 1 — not just against last week.

THE SUNDAY ORATORY LAB — FIXED STRUCTURE (2 HOURS)

This structure stays identical every week. Only the topic and skill change. Don't redesign the format — repetition of the format is what builds the habit.

## Tables from the source document

### Source table 1

Week 1 | First Rep Get on camera. Learn hooks and structure. The only goal is a complete recording, not a good one.
Weeks 2–3 | Hand Gestures Open palm, counting, direction, comparison, contrast, emphasis. Gestures will feel forced before they feel natural.
Week 4 | Voice Modulation Loud/soft, fast/slow, emphasis. This is what keeps dry topics from sounding like a textbook.
Week 5 | Pauses & Silence Dead air to you, breathing room to the listener. Count to two before continuing.
Week 6 | Storytelling Setting, conflict, resolution, moral — and staying balanced on a topic that invites strong opinions.
Week 7 | Stage Movement Purposeful movement between 3 zones. You're past the halfway mark — continuity over a clean streak.
Week 8 | Eye Contact Left / center / right audience, held 2–3 seconds each. Use real specifics from your own life.
Week 9 | Persuasion Logic, examples, emotion, credibility — and making the logical case stand on its own.
Week 10 | Explaining Simply Simplification and analogies. Clarity, not persuasion — different skill, different goal.
Week 11 | Counterarguments Present both sides, argue against your own point. Discomfort here is the actual training.
Week 12 | Charisma & Presence Natural smile, relaxed posture, conversational tone. Integration, not a new add-on.
Week 13 | Full Integration Every skill from weeks 1–12, in one performance. Judge it against the whole arc, not just Week 1.

Oratory Skill | Speech Structure & Hooks

### Source table 5

12:30 – 1:00 PM Learn the skill | Watch 3 short, well-regarded speeches (TED-style or similar) back to back. Each time, ignore the content — track only 3 things: how it opens, what the core argument is, how it closes. Write down, in one line each, the opening line / hook used by all 3 speakers. YouTube search: “best speech openings examples” / “how to write a hook for a speech” / “TED talk opening lines breakdown”
1:00 – 1:30 PM Write the speech | Topic: "Who Am I Without Marks?" — write your own content, but force the shape: Hook → Introduction → 3 Main Points → Story/Example → Conclusion. Spend the first 5 minutes only on the hook. Try 2–3 versions before settling on one. Make sure each of the 3 main points has one clear sentence summarizing it — if you can't summarize it in one line, it's not focused enough yet.
1:30 – 1:45 PM First recording | Record once, full energy, no stopping. Don't worry about the hook landing perfectly — this is about completing the rep.
1:45 – 2:00 PM Review & score | Watch the recording once without judging. Then score yourself out of 10 on: Structure, Confidence, Gestures, Voice, Persuasion. Since this week's skill is structure and hooks, look specifically at: did the opening actually hook, or did you just start talking? Identify ONE biggest mistake — likely related to the hook or a weak transition.
2:00 – 2:20 PM Fix session | Drill only the weak part. If the hook was flat, rewrite and re-say just the hook 5–6 times until it has punch. If a transition was clunky, practice just that one transition line in isolation. YouTube search: “how to fix a weak speech opening”
2:20 – 2:30 PM Final recording | Record the full speech again. Compare Recording 1 vs Recording 2 — specifically, does the hook land better this time?

### Source table 7

Oratory Skill | Hand Gestures Part 1

### Source table 8

12:30 – 1:00 PM Learn the skill | Study open palm gestures (used for honesty/openness), counting gestures (for listing points), and direction gestures (pointing to a time, place, or idea). Watch for the most common mistake: gestures that are too small, too fast, or repeated without meaning (fidgeting vs. gesturing). YouTube search: “hand gestures for public speaking tutorial” / “open palm gesture public speaking” / “counting gestures while speaking”
1:00 – 1:30 PM Write the speech | Topic: Ancient India — Mauryas, Guptas, Cholas, India's Golden Age. Use the same shape: Hook → Introduction → 3 Main Points → Story/Example → Conclusion. As you write, mark 3–4 spots in the script where an open-palm, counting, or direction gesture would naturally fit (e.g. "three great empires" = counting gesture).
1:30 – 1:45 PM First recording | Record once at full energy. Use the gestures you marked, even if they feel forced — force them anyway this week.
1:45 – 2:00 PM Review & score | Score yourself out of 10 on Structure, Confidence, Gestures, Voice, Persuasion. Watch with sound off once, just to see if your gestures support what you're saying or look random. Identify ONE biggest gesture mistake.
2:00 – 2:20 PM Fix session | Drill only the gesture issue. Stand in front of a mirror or camera and practice the 3 gesture types in isolation — palm open, counting on fingers, pointing to a direction — without speaking, then add the words back in. YouTube search: “practice hand gestures speaking mirror drill”
2:20 – 2:30 PM Final recording | Record the full speech again. Compare Recording 1 vs Recording 2 — do the gestures look intentional now instead of forced?

Oratory Skill | Hand Gestures Part 2

### Source table 11

12:30 – 1:00 PM Learn the skill | Study comparison gestures (hands showing two sides), contrast gestures (one hand up, one down, or side to side), and emphasis gestures (a sharp, single motion on the key word). 1:45 – 2:00 PM Review & score | Score yourself out of 10 on Structure, Confidence, Gestures, Voice, Persuasion. Check specifically: did the comparison gesture happen on the comparison word, or before/after it? Identify ONE biggest mistake.
2:00 – 2:20 PM Fix session | Drill only the weak gesture. Since you have extra time on content this week, spend it here — repeat the comparison/contrast lines with the gesture until the timing is exact. YouTube search: “gesture timing with words speech practice”
2:20 – 2:30 PM Final recording | Record again. Compare Recording 1 vs Recording 2 — is the gesture now landing exactly on the word it supports?

Oratory Skill | Voice Modulation

### Source table 14

12:30 – 1:00 PM Learn the skill | Study loud voice, soft voice, fast pace, slow pace, and emphasis words — how speakers change at least one of these every 15–20 seconds to avoid sounding flat. YouTube search: “voice modulation public speaking tutorial” / “vocal variety speech training” / “how to emphasize words speaking”
1:00 – 1:30 PM Write the speech | Topic: Capitalism vs Socialism. This is naturally dry to explain simply — mark 3–4 lines where you'll deliberately shift tone (loud for a strong claim, slow for a key definition).
1:30 – 1:45 PM First recording | Record once. Exaggerate the tone shifts more than feels comfortable — voice modulation is what keeps this topic from sounding like a textbook.
1:45 – 2:00 PM Review & score | Score yourself out of 10 on Structure, Confidence, Gestures, Voice, Persuasion. Listen with eyes closed if possible — does your voice ever change, or does it stay one flat tone? Identify ONE biggest mistake.
2:00 – 2:20 PM Fix session | Drill only the voice issue. Read one paragraph of your speech using three different emotional tones (serious, urgent, conversational) back to back. YouTube search: “read same paragraph different tones exercise” / “vocal variety drill speakers”
2:20 – 2:30 PM Final recording | Record again. Compare Recording 1 vs Recording 2 — can you hear at least 2 clear tone shifts now?
